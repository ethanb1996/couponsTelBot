# Roadmap

## Roadmap Principles
- Trust before scale
- Compliance before automation
- Manual-first learning before systems complexity
- Narrow category focus before inventory expansion
- Monetization through low-risk channels before direct commerce

## Phase 0: Validation

### Goals
- Validate that Israeli users want a Telegram-native savings product
- Learn whether fast-food and adjacent categories are the strongest entry wedge
- Test whether users respond to a smaller set of verified offers
- Prove that the team can source and publish offers through compliant manual workflows
- Measure early trust signals before building heavier product infrastructure

### Features
- Telegram channel or lightweight bot presence
- basic onboarding and product explanation
- manually curated offer posts in a standardized format
- source labeling such as affiliate, partner, sponsored, or curated public offer
- manual offer verification checklist
- basic click tracking and post-level engagement measurement
- lightweight user feedback collection such as "worked" or "didn't work"
- simple operator workflow for pausing or removing bad offers

### Risks
- false positive demand if users join but do not actually engage with offers
- low trust if even a small number of early offers are stale or unclear
- supply fragility if sourcing depends on one merchant or one informal partner
- operational overload if manual curation takes too much time per offer
- Telegram distribution risk if acquisition relies on spammy tactics

### Why It Comes in This Phase
This phase exists to answer the cheapest and safest questions first: do users care, do they trust the format, and can the team produce a steady stream of valid offers without violating platform or merchant constraints. It deliberately avoids checkout, resale, and automation so the business can learn before taking on legal or operational complexity.

## Phase 1: MVP

### Goals
- Turn the validation loop into a repeatable product experience
- Improve user onboarding, preference capture, and offer relevance
- Formalize internal operations for verification, publishing, support, and takedowns
- Establish the first repeatable low-risk monetization path
- Reduce invalid-offer rates and support burden enough to sustain a small-team launch

### Features
- Telegram bot, channel, or hybrid delivery surface with guided onboarding
- category preferences and lightweight personalization
- curated offer feed with merchant name, expiry, restrictions, and redemption steps
- explicit source and disclosure labels on every offer
- internal admin workflow to add, edit, pause, expire, and review offers
- invalid-offer report queue and support response flow
- analytics for views, clicks, repeat engagement, source performance, and defect rates
- sponsored placements or affiliate/referral attribution where permitted
- supplier quality tracking and audit trail for offer provenance

### Risks
- user expectations may outrun the product if they expect checkout or guaranteed redemption
- support load may grow if more users expose weak supply sources
- monetization may remain too thin if affiliate and sponsorship economics are weak
- manual operations may become a bottleneck before internal tools are ready
- supplier concentration may remain high even if user demand is strong

### Why It Comes in This Phase
Once validation shows promise, the next step is not aggressive scale. It is to make the safe version of the business consistent and measurable. This phase adds enough product and ops structure to support a real launch while still preserving the manual, compliant control points that protect trust and reduce risk.

## Phase 2: Growth

### Goals
- Expand user growth using channels and categories that performed well in MVP
- Increase offer relevance, repeat usage, and monetization efficiency
- Reduce manual overhead with compliant internal tooling rather than risky external automation
- Broaden supply through repeatable partner and affiliate relationships
- Build a more resilient operating model with lower supplier concentration

### Features
- improved segmentation by category, user preference, and engagement patterns
- more refined recommendation or targeting logic inside Telegram flows
- stronger content templates and experiments for conversion optimization
- expanded partner onboarding and campaign management workflows for approved suppliers
- internal tooling for faster verification, scheduling, expiry management, and reporting
- richer monetization support for sponsored campaigns, affiliate optimization, and lead-quality reporting
- retention loops such as saved offers, reminders, or re-engagement prompts where appropriate
- broader but still controlled category expansion beyond the initial wedge

### Risks
- growth pressure may tempt the team into lower-quality or less-compliant supply
- expanding categories too fast may dilute trust and operational focus
- increased personalization or automation may introduce privacy, moderation, or quality issues
- partner growth may create commercial pressure to publish offers that are not yet well verified
- Telegram may become a limiting channel if distribution diversification lags

### Why It Comes in This Phase
Growth should happen only after the team has a working trust loop, a repeatable supply process, and at least one credible monetization path. This phase scales what already works and automates internal operations safely, without crossing into prohibited sourcing or ambiguous direct commerce.

## Phase 3: Defensibility

### Goals
- Create advantages that are hard for generic deal channels to copy
- deepen supply-side relationships and secure higher-quality inventory access
- strengthen trust, brand credibility, and user retention
- determine whether any higher-complexity commerce model is justified by evidence
- move from a useful Telegram product to a durable category business

### Features
- exclusive or preferred partner relationships with approved merchants or distributors
- stronger brand, trust, and disclosure systems
- higher-quality offer provenance and supplier performance standards
- deeper analytics on category economics, user retention, and partner ROI
- structured partner tools for approved campaign submission and reporting
- loyalty or retention mechanisms built on verified value delivery
- compliance reviews and phase-gates for any future managed inventory or resale model
- only if legally cleared and operationally justified: tightly scoped pilots for direct commerce with explicit transfer rights and strong fraud controls

### Risks
- premature pursuit of exclusivity may distract from core user value
- deeper commercial relationships may increase pressure to compromise neutrality or trust
- any move toward direct payments or managed inventory reintroduces major refund, fraud, and legal exposure
- a moat built on weak supplier contracts or brand claims may not hold
- expansion beyond Telegram may require a new product and acquisition strategy

### Why It Comes in This Phase
Defensibility only matters after the core loop is proven. Exclusive supply, stronger retention systems, and possible commerce expansion are valuable only if the business already has trusted distribution, compliant operations, and evidence that the market wants more than curated discovery. This phase keeps higher-risk bets gated behind proof, legal review, and operational maturity.

## Phase Progression Logic
The roadmap intentionally moves from lowest-risk learning to higher-complexity execution:

1. Phase 0 proves demand and trust cheaply.
2. Phase 1 makes the safe MVP repeatable.
3. Phase 2 scales only the compliant loops that already work.
4. Phase 3 adds defensibility and explores higher-complexity models only if the earlier phases justify it.
